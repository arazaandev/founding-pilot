# Lancara Offer

Last updated: 7 August 2026

Lancara turns scattered company evidence into a short, human-reviewed set of accounts that a B2B service firm can evaluate and approach with context.

For each account, Lancara shows what was observed, why the company fits the customer's services, why the timing may matter, what remains uncertain, which stakeholders may be relevant, and what the sales team should verify or do next.

Today, Lancara is a founder-led research service supported by software and AI-assisted workflows. Final evidence verification, qualification, interpretation, and outreach recommendations remain human-reviewed.

## Current offer at a glance

Give Lancara a clear ICP, commercial requirements, and one 30-minute configuration session. Within five business days after complete onboarding, receive three human-reviewed opportunity briefs showing which accounts deserve attention, why they fit, what evidence supports the timing, which stakeholders may matter, and what to do next. Then review the briefs together in 20 minutes before deciding whether recurring delivery deserves a paid test.

The customer does not need to buy software, configure a workflow, or commit to a paid pilot before reviewing real output.

## Validation status

Lancara is currently at **Gate A - Messaging discovery**. This document defines the offer being tested; it does not show that customers want it, use it, or will pay for it.

The immediate evidence required is qualified customer conversation. Free Opportunity Calibration tests the usefulness of real briefs at Gate B. The Four-Week Founding Pilot tests payment at Gate C. Continued payment, renewal, continuation, or referral tests repeatability at Gate D.

## Core promise

Lancara helps customers answer:

1. Which companies should we prioritize?
2. Why are they relevant to our business?
3. What evidence suggests they may need our services?
4. Why may the timing be important now?
5. Which stakeholders should we approach?
6. What should our sales team do next?

The objective is not to produce the largest possible lead list.

The objective is to give the customer a smaller set of researched accounts with enough evidence and commercial context to make a defensible next decision.

## The practical value

The unit of value is not a lead. It is a defensible account decision: contact, research further, monitor, postpone, or exclude.

| Without Lancara | What Lancara delivers | Decision the customer can make |
|---|---|---|
| A broad list of company names | Accounts filtered against the customer's services, target market, exclusions, and commercial requirements | Which companies deserve further attention |
| Evidence scattered across websites, announcements, vacancies, and company profiles | Dated source evidence separated from Lancara's interpretation | Why the account may be relevant and how reliable the reasoning is |
| A single signal treated as buyer intent | A combined opportunity pattern, alternative explanations, and missing information | Whether to approach, research further, monitor, postpone, or exclude |
| Generic outreach based on surface-level personalization | Relevant stakeholders, conversation objectives, verification questions, and a recommended next action | How to begin a better-prepared commercial conversation |
| Research assembled separately by individual salespeople | A consistent brief that the team can approve, edit, or reject | Where the team should spend its limited research and outreach time |

These are intended workflow benefits, not validated customer outcomes. Lancara has not yet proven that customers save meaningful time, contact the recommended accounts, create pipeline, or pay for continued delivery.

## What the customer provides

Lancara needs enough customer-specific context to avoid producing a generic lead list:

- Services and strongest capabilities
- Target industries, company types, locations, and deal requirements
- Accounts and situations to exclude
- Relevant case studies or proof of delivery
- Existing customers, active prospects, and conflicts where applicable
- A decision-maker who can review and reject the output honestly

## What each opportunity brief must clarify

- **Account:** Which company deserves attention?
- **Fit:** Why is it relevant to this customer's services and commercial requirements?
- **Evidence:** What was observed, where, and when?
- **Interpretation:** What need or opportunity might the evidence indicate?
- **Timing:** Why might the situation matter now?
- **Confidence:** How complete and reliable is the evidence?
- **Uncertainty:** What is missing, what may be wrong, and what could disqualify the account?
- **Stakeholders:** Who may own, influence, or evaluate the relevant decision?
- **Action:** Should the customer contact, research, monitor, postpone, or exclude the account?
- **Preparation:** What should the salesperson verify or ask before making a claim?

## Who the current offer is for

During Phase 1.5, Lancara is testing the offer with two customer groups:

- Specialist recruitment agencies
- Software and IT service firms

A strong calibration customer:

- Depends on winning new company clients
- Has a defined specialization, target market, or commercial focus
- Earns enough from one successful client to justify paid opportunity research
- Currently relies on referrals, fragmented research, broad lists, or inconsistent account prioritization
- Can provide customer-specific inputs and relevant exclusions
- Has a founder, director, or commercial leader who can review the briefs
- Can act on an approved account and report what happened
- Does not already have a mature internal account-intelligence system

The current offer is not designed for consumer businesses, pure self-service SaaS companies, freelance collectives, large global firms with mature intelligence operations, or companies unable or unwilling to approach selected accounts.

Each customer is intended to use an industry-specific and service-specific opportunity-intelligence playbook.

### 1. Customer and ICP intelligence

Lancara's customer-specific model is designed to capture how each customer wins business, including:

- Services offered
- Target industries
- Target company characteristics
- Geographic coverage
- Preferred deal size
- Commercial requirements
- Excluded accounts
- Buyer personas
- Relevant case studies
- Successful previous customers
- Competitive strengths
- Delivery capacity
- Existing customers and active prospects

Lancara does not rely only on broad filters such as industry, company size, or location.

Its qualification approach is designed around:

- Business fit
- Problem relevance
- Timing
- Commercial potential
- Customer capability
- Evidence quality
- Actionability

During the current phase, these inputs and rules are gathered and applied manually. A future platform may store and operationalize validated versions of them.

### 2. Total addressable market sourcing

The planned platform will help customers build and maintain a relevant account universe.

Future capabilities may include:

- Sourcing companies from approved public and connected data sources
- Importing customer-provided account lists
- Normalizing company names, domains, industries, and locations
- Detecting duplicate accounts
- Excluding existing customers and unsuitable companies
- Categorizing accounts using customer-specific definitions
- Identifying new companies entering the customer's target market
- Refreshing the qualified account universe on an agreed schedule

The intended result is not a static database. It is a customer-specific view of the market that can change as companies, evidence, and customer priorities change.

Recurring refresh and connected-source collection are planned capabilities, not current operational claims.

### 3. Signal detection and opportunity patterns

Lancara's research process is designed to evaluate evidence that may indicate a company is experiencing a relevant business need.

Possible signals include:

- Hiring activity
- Repeated or difficult-to-fill vacancies
- Leadership changes
- Funding events
- Geographic expansion
- New offices or facilities
- Product launches
- Technology initiatives
- Regulatory or compliance changes
- Mergers and acquisitions
- Rapid headcount growth
- New business units
- Procurement activity
- Public tenders
- Technology-stack changes
- Customer-specific operational events

Lancara does not treat one signal as confirmed buying intent.

It combines related evidence into opportunity patterns. Examples may include:

- Specialist hiring bottleneck
- Rapid team expansion
- New-market buildout
- Leadership-led transformation
- Technology-modernization initiative
- Regulatory-readiness requirement
- Outsourcing-capacity gap
- Post-funding operational expansion

Each pattern should be evaluated against the customer's services and commercial requirements. During validation, this interpretation remains manual and human-reviewed. The planned platform may later assist with recurring detection and draft classification.

### 4. Account research and qualification

During validation, human-reviewed research can transform fragmented company information into structured commercial intelligence.

For a relevant account, an opportunity brief may include:

- Company profile
- Business context
- Verified source evidence
- Signal history
- Opportunity pattern
- Customer-specific fit assessment
- Probable business problem
- Relevant customer service
- Timing and urgency
- Commercial opportunity assessment
- Evidence-confidence level
- Potential disqualifying factors
- Questions requiring further verification
- Recommended monitoring or action status

Possible recommendations include:

- Contact now
- Research further
- Monitor
- Postpone
- Exclude

Commercial opportunity potential must remain separate from evidence confidence. Any future scoring model must remain explainable.

Users should be able to see:

- Which dimensions influenced the result
- Which evidence supports the conclusion
- What remains uncertain
- Why the account was prioritized
- What could make the recommendation incorrect

Low-confidence evidence must not produce an unsupported contact-now recommendation.

Human-reviewed briefs may recommend:

Workflow orchestration is a planned platform capability. It is intended to connect approved opportunity intelligence to a customer's existing sales workflow.

Future capabilities may include:

- Sales-representative assignment
- Weekly opportunity digests
- CRM enrichment
- CRM updates
- Spreadsheet import and export
- Webhooks

The CRM can remain the system of record for:

- Contacts

- Deals
- Pipeline stages
- Proposals
- Tasks
- Revenue

The planned Lancara platform can become the intelligence and qualification layer that helps determine what should enter the sales workflow and why. CRM synchronization, dashboards, and connected activation are not currently operational.

## Lancara playbooks

Lancara is designed to support customers through ready-to-run playbooks rather than requiring them to build complex workflows from scratch.

During validation, a playbook is a customer-specific manual research and qualification specification. Future software may operationalize playbooks only after their rules have been tested with customers.

Each playbook defines:

- Target account universe
- Relevant services
- Signal sources
- Opportunity patterns
- Qualification rules
- Evidence requirements
- Exclusions
- Stakeholders
- Recommended actions
- Feedback rules
- Success metrics

Potential playbooks include:

### Recruitment Hiring Demand Playbook

Identifies companies showing credible specialist hiring demand and evaluates whether external recruitment support may be relevant.

### Software Project Opportunity Playbook

Identifies companies showing expansion, technology, operational, or leadership signals that may create demand for software-development services.

### Cybersecurity Readiness Playbook

Identifies companies facing regulatory, infrastructure, leadership, or incident-related conditions that may create demand for cybersecurity support.

### Cloud Modernization Playbook

Identifies organizations showing infrastructure growth, migration, hiring, or digital-transformation signals.

### Data and AI Transformation Playbook

Identifies companies investing in data teams, AI initiatives, analytics infrastructure, or related leadership.

### Account Expansion Playbook

Monitors existing customers and product users for cross-sell, upsell, geographic-expansion, or new-stakeholder opportunities.

### Champion and Leadership Tracking Playbook

Tracks important contacts and leadership changes across target and former customer accounts.

### Inbound Qualification Playbook

Enriches and evaluates inbound enquiries, routes them to the correct team, and prepares a relevant first response.

These examples describe possible playbooks. They are not all implemented or validated.

## Commercial model

### Current validation offers

#### Free Opportunity Calibration

A no-cost output test that lets a qualified customer judge Lancara using real, customer-specific research before considering a paid engagement.

**Customer contribution**

- Complete onboarding information about services, ICP, exclusions, case studies, and commercial requirements
- One 30-minute configuration session
- One decision-maker able to review the briefs and explain approvals or rejections

**Lancara delivery**

- Three researched, human-reviewed opportunity briefs
- Delivery within five business days after complete onboarding
- One 20-minute review session
- No payment

**Decision enabled**

The customer can approve, edit, or reject each account; state whether it is relevant enough to contact; identify missing evidence; and decide whether recurring opportunity research deserves a paid test.

This calibration does not promise that an account is buying, that outreach will receive a reply, or that a meeting or project will result.

#### Four-Week Founding Pilot

A four-week paid test of whether Lancara can repeatedly produce opportunity briefs the customer considers relevant enough to use, at a delivery cost that can become sustainable.

The current test is Rp4 million, requested upfront, for:

- One configured playbook
- 10–15 accounts researched weekly
- 5–8 complete human-reviewed briefs weekly
- Weekly calibration
- Up to two participating customer team members
- A maximum of two simultaneous paid customers

Payment is requested only after the customer has reviewed real calibration output and agreed that continued delivery is worth testing. The maximum of two simultaneous paid customers is a delivery-capacity constraint, not manufactured scarcity.

The price does not include automated outreach, CRM synchronization, a customer dashboard, contact-data waterfalls, or guaranteed replies, meetings, pipeline, revenue, placements, or projects.

**What the pilot is designed to establish**

- Which account types and opportunity patterns the customer approves or rejects
- Which evidence the customer trusts before outreach
- Whether approved accounts are used in real sales activity
- Whether recurring briefs reduce avoidable research work
- Whether the customer will pay for continued delivery
- Whether founder time and variable research cost support a sustainable service

This offer is a commercial validation test, not proof of established demand or repeatability.

#### Platform Subscription

#### Enterprise Implementation
